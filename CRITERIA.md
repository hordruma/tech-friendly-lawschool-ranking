# Tech-Friendly Law School Ranking — Public Methodology

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last updated:** 2026-06-04  
**Maintained by:** The Tech-Friendly Law School Ranking Project

---

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This ranking assesses law schools globally on their genuine commitment to legal technology, artificial intelligence, and technology-integrated legal practice. The ranking is designed for:

- Prospective students seeking a technology-forward legal education
- Legal employers evaluating the tech-readiness of graduates
- Researchers studying legal education reform
- Law schools seeking a structured framework for self-assessment

We rank law schools globally. We do not treat US ABA-accredited schools as a default baseline. A law school in Singapore, India, or Germany is assessed by the same criteria as one in the United States.

---

## 2. What We Measure and Why

Modern legal practice is being reshaped by artificial intelligence, document automation, data analytics, and new legal delivery models. We believe that a law school's genuine engagement with technology is measurable through concrete, verifiable evidence — not press releases.

We measure **four substantive dimensions** plus a **press release gap** accountability mechanism for the **Tech-Friendliness Score**. Alongside it, we compute an independent **Practical Skills Score** assessing how well schools prepare students for real practice. These two scores, together with an aggregated **Prestige Score** derived from external rankings, feed the **Meta-Rank**.

### Tech-Friendliness Score (0–100)

| Dimension | Points | Rationale |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Curriculum | 40 | What students actually learn is the core product of a law school |
| Infrastructure | 30 | Sustained institutional investment signals long-term commitment |
| Faculty & Research | 20 | Intellectual leadership drives curriculum and produces knowledge |
| Community & Career | 10 | Student outcomes are the ultimate measure |
| Press Release Gap (penalty) | −5 to −20 | Accountability for marketed-but-discontinued programs |

### Practical Skills Score (0–100, independent dimension)

| Sub-dimension | Points | Rationale |
|---------------|--------|-----------|
| Clinical programs | 0–25 | Live-client clinics are the clearest signal of practical training |
| Skills curriculum | 0–25 | Structured simulation and advocacy training |
| Experiential placements | 0–25 | Externships and field placements extend learning into real practice |
| Professional readiness | 0–25 | Pro bono requirements, bar support, and employer partnerships |

### Meta-Rank Weights

| Signal | Weight | Rationale |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Tech-Friendliness Score | 50% | Our primary unique contribution |
| Practical Skills Score | 30% | How well schools prepare students for real practice |
| Prestige Score | 20% | Contextual background already covered by other rankings |

## 3. Detailed Scoring Rubric

### 3.1 Curriculum (40 points)

#### 3.1.1 LegalTech / AI Courses (max 20 points)

Points are awarded per verified course in the current catalog:
- **Required** course: **2 points**
- **Elective** course: **1 point**
- Maximum: 20 points

Qualifying courses must:
- Appear in the current (verified within 18 months) course catalog or schedule
- Have substantial technology content (not merely mention technology in passing)
- Count towards the JD or qualifying LLM degree

Topic areas that qualify: artificial intelligence & law, legal technology, document automation, e-discovery, cybersecurity law (with practical tech component), data privacy (with technical component), computational legal analysis, legal design, blockchain & law (with practical component), coding for lawyers.

Topic areas that do **not** qualify by themselves: general IP law, internet law without practical tech, legal research (unless explicitly AI-enhanced).

#### 3.1.2 Practical Skills Requirement (0–10 points)

- 10 pts: Formal, required practical technology component in the curriculum (e.g., required tech practicum, required coding module)
- 7 pts: Strongly encouraged practical requirement with dedicated tech content
- 4 pts: Practical skills requirement exists but technology content is incidental
- 0 pts: No formal practical technology requirement

#### 3.1.3 Tech-Integrated Clinics (0–10 points)

- 10 pts: Dedicated legaltech clinic (e.g., access-to-justice tech clinic, startup law clinic with tech focus) with verified enrollment
- 7 pts: Established clinic where technology delivery is a primary component
- 4 pts: Traditional clinic with documented technology integration
- 0 pts: No tech-integrated clinical offerings

---

### 3.2 Infrastructure (30 points)

#### 3.2.1 Dedicated LegalTech Center or Institute (0–10 points)

- 10 pts: Active center with dedicated staff, physical space, regular programming (events, workshops, competitions), and public web presence updated within 12 months
- 7 pts: Active center with some of the above but partial evidence
- 4 pts: Named center or institute with limited documented activity
- 0 pts: No dedicated center, or center exists only in name (press release gap candidate)

#### 3.2.2 Joint Degrees (0–10 points, 5 points each)

Qualifying joint degrees: JD/CS, JD/Data Science, JD/Information Science, JD/Engineering (any), JD/MBA with Technology track, or equivalent in other jurisdictions.

- 5 pts per qualifying joint degree pathway, up to 10 pts total
- Must be formally offered and appear in current admissions materials

#### 3.2.3 Industry Partnerships (0–10 points)

Formal partnerships with technology companies, legaltech startups, or innovation-focused legal organizations:
- 10 pts: 3+ documented active partnerships with named organizations, with verifiable joint activities (placement programs, co-taught courses, sponsored research)
- 7 pts: 2 documented active partnerships
- 4 pts: 1 documented active partnership
- 0 pts: No documented partnerships, or partnerships only listed in historical press releases

---

### 3.3 Faculty & Research (20 points)

#### 3.3.1 Faculty with Technology Expertise (0–10 points)

Full-time faculty (tenure-track, clinical faculty, or long-term visiting) whose primary or significant secondary expertise involves technology law, AI, computational methods, or legal technology:
- 10 pts: 4 or more such faculty
- 7 pts: 3 such faculty
- 4 pts: 2 such faculty
- 2 pts: 1 such faculty
- 0 pts: None

Practitioner or adjunct faculty do not count toward this criterion unless they hold a substantial appointment.

#### 3.3.2 Active LegalTech Research Output (0–10 points)

Peer-reviewed publications, working papers, or high-impact reports on legal technology topics within the last 3 years:
- 10 pts: 5+ verified outputs
- 7 pts: 3–4 verified outputs
- 4 pts: 1–2 verified outputs
- 0 pts: No verified recent output

---

### 3.4 Community & Career (10 points)

#### 3.4.1 Student LegalTech Organizations (0–5 points)

- 5 pts: 2 or more active student organizations with documented recent activity (events, website, social presence within 12 months)
- 3 pts: 1 active student organization
- 0 pts: No active student legaltech organizations

#### 3.4.2 Career Placement in LegalTech (0–5 points)

- 5 pts: Documented placement data or named employer relationships with legaltech companies, courts using tech, or other tech-forward legal employers
- 3 pts: Anecdotal evidence of legaltech placement from school materials
- 0 pts: No documented legaltech career focus

---

### 3.5 Press Release Gap (−5 to −20 points)

The **Press Release Gap** is this ranking's most important accountability mechanism.

A Press Release Gap (PRG) occurs when a law school has publicly marketed a program, center, course, or initiative that **no longer exists or no longer functions as described** — and the school continues to benefit reputationally from the original announcement without disclosing the change.

This is distinct from normal program evolution. A PRG requires:
1. A documented public claim (press release, website, admissions material)
2. Current evidence that the program no longer exists or is substantially different
3. A temporal gap — the claim was made, then quietly removed or abandoned

**Penalty scale:**

| Severity | Points | Criteria |
|----------|--------|----------|
| Minor | −5 | 1 gap; small program; claim removed but not currently circulated |
| Moderate | −10 | 1–2 gaps; medium-visibility programs; still referenced in admissions or rankings submissions |
| Major | −15 | Multiple gaps; flagship programs; actively marketed despite absence |
| Egregious | −20 | Systematic pattern; evidence of deliberate misrepresentation; gaps directly affecting applicant decisions |

PRG deductions are cumulative with a floor of −20 total.

## 3.6 Practical Skills Score (0–100, independent dimension)

The **Practical Skills Score** is a fully independent score from the Tech-Friendliness Score. It measures how well a law school prepares students for the realities of legal practice through clinical programs, skills curricula, experiential placements, and professional readiness initiatives.

This score feeds the Meta-Rank at 30% weight (see Section 9).

### 3.6.1 Clinical Programs (0–25 points)

Points are awarded per distinct live-client clinic with a verified current enrollment:

- **Each distinct live-client clinic:** 4 points (maximum 20 points)
- **Required clinical participation:** +5 bonus points (if at least one clinic is mandatory for all JD students)

A clinic qualifies if it involves actual client representation or direct service, as distinct from simulations. Virtual or limited-scope clinics count if they involve real clients.

### 3.6.2 Skills Curriculum (0–25 points)

- **Required skills/simulation courses:** 3 points each (maximum 15 points)
  - Qualifying courses: legal writing (advanced), negotiation, client counseling, trial advocacy, drafting, professional responsibility with practical component
- **Moot court / negotiation / advocacy programs:** 2 points each (maximum 10 points)
  - Qualifying: interscholastic moot court teams, intra-school competitions with coaching, structured negotiation programs

### 3.6.3 Experiential Placements (0–25 points)

- **Structured externship program:** up to 15 points
  - 15 pts: comprehensive externship program with multiple placement tracks, faculty supervision, and academic integration
  - 10 pts: structured externship program with supervision but limited academic integration
  - 5 pts: informal or limited externship opportunities
- **Field placement / co-op programs:** up to 10 points
  - 10 pts: dedicated field placement or co-op program with employer partnerships and academic credit
  - 5 pts: field placement opportunities without dedicated program infrastructure

### 3.6.4 Professional Readiness (0–25 points)

- **Pro bono requirement:**
  - Mandatory (enforced hours requirement): 10 points
  - Encouraged (stated goal, not enforced): 5 points
  - None: 0 points
- **Bar passage support / skills bridge:** up to 8 points
  - Structured bar preparation program or skills bridge course with academic credit: 8 points
  - Informal bar support resources or workshops: 4 points
  - None: 0 points
- **Career integration (employer partnerships for practical skills):** up to 7 points
  - Documented relationships with legal employers specifically for practical skills development (not just placement): 7 points
  - General career services without skills-specific employer partnerships: 3 points
  - None: 0 points

**Total Practical Skills Score: 0–100**

## 4. Ranking Tiers

| Tier | Score Range | Meaning |
|------|-------------|---------|
| S | 85–100 | Exemplary: genuine leader in legal technology education |
| A | 70–84 | Strong: substantial and well-integrated technology focus |
| B | 50–69 | Developing: meaningful commitment with room for growth |
| C | 30–49 | Limited: minimal technology integration despite some presence |
| D | Below 30 | Absent or declining: little to no genuine technology engagement |
| Unranked | N/A | Insufficient verified data to assign a score |

## 5. Data Collection and Verification

### 5.1 Sources

All claims must be traceable to a source URL with a verification date. We use three tiers:

1. **Primary sources** (highest weight): The school's official website, current course catalog, official faculty pages, regulatory filings
2. **Secondary sources** (verified): Third-party news reports, legal publication features, academic databases (SSRN, etc.)
3. **AI-assisted research** (flagged): AI agents are used to surface potential sources, but every AI-sourced claim requires human verification with a primary or secondary source

### 5.2 Research Process

1. An automated research agent (see `/agents/researcher.py`) performs initial reconnaissance using web search, retrieving current course catalogs, faculty pages, and press releases.
2. The agent extracts structured data and flags potential press release gaps by comparing dated public claims against current catalog content.
3. Human reviewers verify all scores and source URLs before publication.
4. Schools are notified of their pending scores and given 30 days to provide corrections with evidence.

### 5.3 Data Freshness

- Scores are considered **current** if all source material was verified within 18 months
- Schools not verified within 18 months are marked **stale** and displayed with reduced confidence
- `last_verified` null means the school record has not been verified by a human reviewer

## 6. How to Submit Corrections

We actively welcome corrections and additions. The ranking is only as good as its data.

### 6.1 For minor corrections
Open a GitHub Issue using the **School Submission / Correction** template. Include:
- The school name and current record as you believe it to be incorrect
- A source URL (must be from a primary or secondary source)
- The date you verified the source

### 6.2 For press release gap reports
Open a GitHub Issue using the **Press Release Gap Report** template. Include:
- The original claim (with URL to archived or current source)
- The current reality (with URL to current catalog or official evidence)
- The approximate year of the original claim
- How the claim is still being circulated

### 6.3 For new schools
Open a GitHub Issue using the **School Submission / Correction** template and select "New School." Completing as many fields as possible will significantly accelerate review.

All accepted corrections are merged via pull request and credited to the contributor in the commit history.

## 7. What This Ranking Is Not

- **Not a general law school ranking.** We do not rank schools on bar passage, employment outcomes, or academic reputation. Other rankings do that.
- **Not a sponsorship-driven ranking.** We do not accept payment from schools for inclusion or improved placement.
- **Not US-centric.** ABA accreditation is noted but not required. We apply jurisdiction-appropriate equivalents for non-US schools.
- **Not a perfect measure.** Smaller schools with excellent technology integration may be disadvantaged by criteria that favor institutional size. We acknowledge this and are actively refining methodology.

---

## 8. Versioning and Methodology Changes

Changes to this methodology document are tracked via git. Major methodology changes that would affect existing scores trigger a re-verification cycle. Schools are notified of methodology changes before scores are updated.

## 9. Meta-Rank Formula

The **Meta-Rank** is a composite ranking that combines three independent signals:

1. **Tech-Friendliness Score (50%)** — our primary unique contribution (0–100, see Sections 3.1–3.5)
2. **Practical Skills Score (30%)** — how well schools prepare students for real legal practice (0–100, see Section 3.6)
3. **Prestige Score (20%)** — an aggregated, normalized score from major external global law school rankings

### Rationale for 50/30/20 weights

- **Tech-friendliness at 50%:** This is our primary signal and our unique analytical contribution. No other ranking measures this. It must dominate.
- **Practical skills at 30%:** Closely related to our mission — we care about schools that prepare graduates for modern practice, not just theory. This dimension rewards schools that bridge knowledge and doing.
- **Prestige at 20%:** Prestige is contextual background that other rankings already handle well. We include it to give users a sense of how tech and practical scores relate to traditional academic standing, but we do not let it dominate.

### Meta-Score Formula

```
meta_score = (tech_score × 0.50) + (practical_skills_score × 0.30) + (prestige_score × 0.20)
```

If `practical_skills_score` is null (not yet researched), the formula falls back to:

```
meta_score = (tech_score × 0.625) + (prestige_score × 0.375)
```

(Proportionally rescaled so the two non-null components sum to 1.0.)

If `prestige_score` is null (no external ranking data), the meta score is null and the school is excluded from meta-ranking.

See `data/meta-ranking/formula.md` for the full normalization specification.
